**MARTA MATE**\

### The problem

Atlanta’s workforce includes many women whose schedules do not match standard 9–5 transit patterns: healthcare workers, hospitality staff, shift workers, students, caregivers, and gig workers. During late-night or early-morning trips, people may face uncertainty, isolation, harassment concerns, transit delays, and anxiety.

The product should not imply that MARTA itself is unsafe or replace professional emergency response. Instead, your framing is:

> **MARTA MATE gives riders more agency during stressful commutes by helping them connect with trusted peers, access discreet safety tools, and regulate anxiety while they wait or travel.**

MARTA already provides official safety reporting, location sharing, anonymous incident reporting, and channels to contact MARTA Police. Your app should clearly surface and complement those official options rather than duplicate or interfere with them.[^1_1][^1_2]

## What to build

Do **not** try to build a complete real-time safety platform. Build a credible, interactive prototype around one complete commuter journey.

### MVP user flow

1. A rider signs in securely.
2. She enters a trip:
    - Starting station or bus stop
    - Destination area
    - Departure window
    - Transit line
    - “I want a walking buddy” or “I want someone in the same rail car”
3. The app finds compatible **Virtual Buddies** using safe, privacy-preserving matching.
4. The rider sees a Buddy Card:
    - First name/alias only
    - Shared route or station
    - Matching time window
    - Shared destination zone, not exact home address
    - “Verified rider” badge
    - Opt-in chat or check-in
5. A transit disruption, lonely wait, or uncomfortable situation prompts the rider to choose:
    - Play a realistic “someone is expecting me” audio
    - Start a grounding exercise
    - Use the official MARTA safety/contact options
    - Share a trip check-in with a trusted contact
6. The post-trip screen asks: “Did you get there safely?” and gives the user a low-friction way to end the session.

### Three core features

| Feature | What the user sees | What you actually implement |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Virtual Buddies** | Match cards for women on compatible lines and time windows | Seeded rider records plus route/time overlap logic |
| **Cover Call** | Buttons such as “Incoming call,” “Security check-in,” and “Friend is waiting” | ElevenLabs-generated audio or pre-generated files with a clear disclaimer |
| **Grounding Canvas** | A calming touch interaction, breathing timer, and short check-in | React animation, haptic vibration where available, and a guided audio clip |
| **Safety Hub** | Official assistance options, quick trip-share link, trusted contact check-in | Prominent UI directing users to official MARTA contacts and emergency services |

### Safety design rules

These details will make your concept more credible to judges:

- Match by **route, station, and time window**, not live precise GPS location.
- Show approximate locations such as “North Avenue Station area” or “same Red Line segment.”
- Use aliases in public matching; reveal further details only after mutual opt-in.
- Make all buddy interactions opt-in and easy to leave or block.
- Include report/block controls.
- Do not claim that the app “prevents assault” or guarantees safety.
- Do not use AI to make emergency decisions.
- Put a persistent emergency/safety route in the interface.
- In your demo, make it explicit that official MARTA Police and emergency resources remain the primary channel for urgent situations. MARTA says riders can report concerns through its app and contact MARTA Police by phone or SMS.[^1_1]

### Why not Solana?

You can force Solana into the product, but it is likely to weaken your story:

- A blockchain-based safety identity or token system creates privacy questions.
- The judges may see it as a sponsor integration with little direct rider benefit.
- It adds wallet setup, transaction design, and security overhead.
- It distracts from the strongest emotional and social value: trusted companionship, discreet support, and calm.

Only add Solana if your team already knows it and can build a narrowly useful feature—such as a privacy-preserving, non-transferable “verified community rider credential.” Even then, do not make it central to the product.

### Backboard: useful but not necessary

Backboard can fit if you make a persistent AI companion that remembers user preferences, such as:

- Preferred station and routes
- Whether the user likes audio or text guidance
- Trusted-contact preferences
- Prior grounding tools that helped
- Preferred commute check-in schedule
- Accessibility preferences

But it is not necessary if Gemini plus a small user profile database already works. Do not add it unless the integration is quick and demonstrable.

### Presage: use carefully

Presage could be a compelling enhancement:

> “With explicit consent, MARTA MATE’s Calm Check can identify a possible rise in stress and offer a grounding exercise or trusted-contact check-in.”

But do **not** claim it can diagnose anxiety, measure danger, infer consent, or determine whether an emergency is happening. Stress signals are sensitive and imperfect. If the SDK takes more than 30–45 minutes to integrate, cut it and protect the core app.

## Sponsor integrations

### Gemini: make it central

Gemini should power a **Commute Calm Coach**, not a generic chatbot.

A rider can type:

> “I’m waiting at Five Points and my train is delayed. I feel nervous because it’s getting late.”

Gemini returns a structured response with:

- A short empathetic acknowledgment
- A “right now” grounding suggestion
- A recommended in-app action, such as “Start a buddy check-in”
- A safety reminder with clear escalation language
- A concise, non-alarmist support message

Gemini’s structured-output capability lets your app request predictable JSON that can directly render a safe UI—for example, `support_message`, `suggested_action`, `grounding_prompt`, `risk_level`, and `emergency_notice`.[^1_3][^1_4]

You can also define safe app functions such as:

```text
findVirtualBuddies(route, station, departureWindow)
createTrustedContactCheckIn(contactId, tripId)
getOfficialSafetyOptions(location)
startGroundingExercise(exerciseType)
```

Gemini function calling lets the model select an app function, but your application—not Gemini—must execute the function and return the result. This is ideal for a guided assistant that can offer a buddy match or a grounding exercise without the model directly taking real-world actions.[^1_5][^1_6]

### Gemini prompt guardrails

Use a system instruction like this:

```text
You are MARTA MATE, a supportive commute companion for adult riders.
Your role is to provide calm, brief, nonjudgmental support and point users
to available in-app safety options.

Never promise safety, diagnose mental health conditions, or claim to contact
emergency services. If the user reports immediate danger, violence, a medical
emergency, or being followed, advise them to call 911 or contact MARTA Police
through the official options displayed in the app.

Avoid asking for an exact home address or unnecessary personal details.
Return concise structured JSON for the app interface.
```

### Auth0: your trust foundation

Auth0 is a simple win for the project’s security and verification story:

- Email/passwordless/social login
- Optional MFA for profile verification
- Protected routes for the matching board
- User identity separated from public buddy aliases
- Persistent session and consent settings

The Auth0 React SDK handles common login, token, renewal, cache, and Universal Login mechanics; it supports `loginWithRedirect` and `loginWithPopup`.[^1_10]

For your pitch:

> “Trust begins before matching. Auth0 protects account access and lets MARTA MATE separate private identity data from the limited profile information riders choose to share.”

## Devpost draft

### Project title

**MARTA MATE — Your calm, connected ride home**

### Inspiration

Women working late shifts often commute when they have fewer transportation options, less predictable transit conditions, and fewer people around them. A delayed train, an unfamiliar station, or an uncomfortable interaction can turn an ordinary ride home into an isolating and stressful experience.

We created MARTA MATE to make late-night commuting feel less lonely and more supported. Rather than replacing emergency services or transit safety systems, MARTA MATE complements them by helping riders connect with peers, access discreet support tools, and regulate stress in the moment.

### What it does

MARTA MATE is a safety and mental-wellness companion for women commuting during nontraditional hours.

Users can:

- Find opt-in Virtual Buddies traveling on the same route during a similar time window
- Request a same-car, walk-to-station, or arrival check-in companion
- Use natural-sounding cover-call and grounding audio when they feel uncomfortable or anxious
- Receive brief, context-aware support from an AI commute companion
- Share trip check-ins with trusted contacts
- Quickly access official safety and emergency options


### How we built it

MARTA MATE uses:

- **React, Vite, TypeScript, and Tailwind CSS** for the responsive web application
- **Auth0** for secure authentication and protected user experiences
- **Google Gemini API** for structured, context-aware commute support and safe action recommendations
- **ElevenLabs** for natural voice-based grounding guidance and comfort audio
- **PostgreSQL/Tiger Data** for optional anonymized, time-series commute check-ins and community demand analytics
- **Vercel** for deployment


### Gemini use

Google Gemini powers MARTA MATE’s Commute Calm Coach. The app provides contextual but privacy-conscious trip information—such as a station area, route, delay context, and user-selected emotional state—and receives structured responses that the UI can safely render.

Instead of open-ended advice, Gemini returns concise support, grounding prompts, and in-app actions such as suggesting a buddy check-in or opening official safety resources. Gemini can produce schema-constrained JSON for reliable UI rendering, while function calling allows the app to connect the assistant to user-approved app functions.[^1_3][^1_5]

### ElevenLabs use

ElevenLabs powers the app’s voice experiences:

- A friendly “someone is expecting me” comfort call
- A neutral commute check-in prompt
- A guided breathing and grounding exercise

The audio experience gives riders a discreet way to feel less isolated without pretending to be emergency services or impersonating real people.

### Challenges we ran into

- Designing peer matching without exposing sensitive location information
- Balancing a supportive AI experience with clear safety boundaries
- Avoiding overclaiming what technology can do in stressful or dangerous situations
- Integrating multiple sponsor technologies while keeping the user flow simple enough for a live demo


### Accomplishments we’re proud of

- Built an end-to-end commuter journey rather than isolated features
- Made privacy and consent visible in the matching experience
- Connected AI support, human connection, and transit-specific safety resources in one flow
- Created a voice-based support experience that feels practical and emotionally considerate


### What we learned

We learned that building for safety requires more than adding an SOS button. It requires careful choices about privacy, language, consent, escalation, and what an app should never promise.

We also learned how structured AI outputs, secure authentication, and voice interfaces can work together to create a product experience that is both helpful and bounded.

### What’s next

- Connect to official transit schedules and service alerts
- Add localized station guidance and accessibility routes
- Add multilingual support
- Partner with employers that have late-shift workers
- Add stronger rider verification and community moderation
- Conduct user research with women who commute during nontraditional hours

## Build target

### Final MVP definition

Build a deployed responsive web app called:

> **MARTA MATE — Your calm, connected ride home.**

A user can:

1. Log in using Auth0.
2. Choose a station/route/time window.
3. View seeded, privacy-safe “Virtual Buddy” matches.
4. Request a match and enter a trip companion state.

6. Play ElevenLabs-powered support audio:

    - A cover-call style audio
    - A grounding exercise

**Build now:**

- Responsive frontend

- One ElevenLabs audio endpoint or pre-generated audio file

**Do not build now:**

- Real-time exact location tracking
- Public map of riders
- Live chat between strangers
- Real emergency calling
- Live MARTA API integration unless it works quickly
- Complex notifications
- Solana wallet onboarding
- Presage biometric analysis
- Full Backboard integration
- Perfect database models and user management

The point is a **complete, believable journey**, not a production safety platform.

### Matching logic

Create `src/lib/matching.ts`:

```ts
import type { BuddyProfile, TripRequest } from "../types/buddy";

const scoreMatch = (buddy: BuddyProfile, request: TripRequest): number => {
  let score = 0;

  if (buddy.route === request.route) score += 50;
  if (buddy.originStationZone === request.originStation) score += 25;
  if (buddy.matchType === request.matchType) score += 15;
  if (buddy.supportPreference === request.comfortPreference) score += 10;

  return score;
};

export const findCompatibleBuddies = (
  buddies: BuddyProfile[],
  request: TripRequest,
): BuddyProfile[] => {
  return [...buddies]
    .filter((buddy) => buddy.status === "available")
    .map((buddy) => ({ buddy, score: scoreMatch(buddy, request) }))
    .filter(({ score }) => score >= 25)
    .sort((a, b) => b.score - a.score)
    .map(({ buddy }) => buddy);
};
```

This gives you actual matching behavior without requiring a live matching backend.

Gemini is one of your strongest prize integrations. Use it for a **structured, bounded Calm Coach**, not an unrestricted chatbot.

Gemini supports structured outputs using JSON Schema, allowing your app to request predictable data for a typed UI rather than parsing free-form text. Gemini also supports function calling to connect model decisions to app functions; your application retains control over actual actions.[^2_2][^2_3][^2_4]

```ts
  }

  const { stationZone, route, delayContext, feeling, preference } = req.body ?? {};

  if (!process.env.GEMINI_API_KEY) {
    return res.status(200).json({
      ...fallbackResponse,
      source: "fallback",

    const ai = new GoogleGenAI({ apiKey: process.env.GEMINI_API_KEY });

- Station area: ${stationZone || "not shared"}

- Do not guarantee safety.

- Do not claim to contact MARTA Police, emergency services, or anyone else.

- Keep all text concise, warm, and actionable.

`;

    const response = await ai.models.generateContent({
      model: "gemini-2.5-flash",
      contents: prompt,
      config: {
        responseMimeType: "application/json",
        responseSchema: {
          type: "object",
          properties: {
            supportMessage: { type: "string" },
            groundingPrompt: { type: "string" },
            suggestedAction: {
              type: "string",
              enum: [
                "start_breathing",
                "play_audio",

                "request_checkin",

              type: "string",

            safetyNotice: { type: "string" },

            "supportMessage",
            "groundingPrompt",
            "suggestedAction",
            "urgency",
            "safetyNotice",
          ],
        },
      },
    });

    const text = response.text ?? JSON.stringify(fallbackResponse);
    const parsed = JSON.parse(text);

    return res.status(200).json({
      ...parsed,
      source: "gemini",
    });
  } catch (error) {
    console.error("Gemini error", error);

    return res.status(200).json({
      ...fallbackResponse,
      source: "fallback",
    });
  }
}
```

### `api/elevenlabs.ts`

```ts
import type { VercelRequest, VercelResponse } from "@vercel/node";

const AUDIO_SCRIPTS: Record<string, string> = {
  friendly_checkin:
    "Hey, I’m checking in because I care about you getting home. Take your time, stay aware of your surroundings, and let me know when you are safely inside.",
  comfort_call:
    "Hey, I’m here. I know your train is delayed, but you are not doing this alone. I’ll stay with you while you wait, and you can check in when you are on your way.",
  grounding_guide:
    "Take a slow breath in for four. Hold for four. Let it out for six. Notice one thing you can see, one thing you can hear, and one thing that helps you feel steady right now.",
};

export default async function handler(
  req: VercelRequest,
  res: VercelResponse,
) {
  if (req.method !== "POST") {
    return res.status(405).json({ error: "Method not allowed" });
  }

  const audioType = req.body?.audioType as string;
  const text = AUDIO_SCRIPTS[audioType];

  if (!text) {
    return res.status(400).json({ error: "Invalid audio type" });
  }

  if (!process.env.ELEVENLABS_API_KEY || !process.env.ELEVENLABS_VOICE_ID) {
    return res.status(200).json({
      source: "fallback",
      audioUrl: `/audio/${audioType.replace("_", "-")}.mp3`,
    });
  }

  try {
    const response = await fetch(
      `https://api.elevenlabs.io/v1/text-to-speech/${process.env.ELEVENLABS_VOICE_ID}`,
      {
        method: "POST",
        headers: {
          "Content-Type": "application/json",
          "xi-api-key": process.env.ELEVENLABS_API_KEY,
          Accept: "audio/mpeg",
        },
        body: JSON.stringify({
          text,
          model_id: "eleven_multilingual_v2",
          voice_settings: {
            stability: 0.55,
            similarity_boost: 0.75,
            style: 0.25,
            use_speaker_boost: true,
          },
        }),
      },
    );

    if (!response.ok) {
      throw new Error("ElevenLabs generation failed");
    }

    const audioBuffer = await response.arrayBuffer();

    res.setHeader("Content-Type", "audio/mpeg");
    res.setHeader("Cache-Control", "no-store");
    return res.status(200).send(Buffer.from(audioBuffer));
  } catch (error) {
    console.error("ElevenLabs error", error);

    return res.status(200).json({
      source: "fallback",
      audioUrl: `/audio/${audioType.replace("_", "-")}.mp3`,
    });
  }
}
```

### Community Pulse concept

Create a page that shows **anonymous, aggregated** trends:

- Late-night support requests by route
- Support preference split: buddy, calming audio, grounding, check-in
- Average self-reported mood score by time block
- Peak commuter support demand

Never show individual riders, exact locations, or exact timestamps.

### `sql/003_tiger_timeseries.sql`

```sql
CREATE MATERIALIZED VIEW IF NOT EXISTS community_pulse_30m
WITH (timescaledb.continuous) AS
SELECT
  time_bucket('30 minutes', created_at) AS bucket,
  route,
  support_type,
  COUNT(*) AS request_count,
  ROUND(AVG(mood_score), 2) AS average_mood_score
FROM community_checkins
GROUP BY bucket, route, support_type
WITH NO DATA;

SELECT add_continuous_aggregate_policy(
  'community_pulse_30m',
  start_offset => INTERVAL '7 days',
  end_offset => INTERVAL '15 minutes',
  schedule_interval => INTERVAL '15 minutes'
);
```

If the continuous aggregate setup causes errors because of free-tier plan differences, simplify to a normal aggregate query. You still qualify if Tiger Data stores and serves your time-stamped check-ins meaningfully.

### `api/community-pulse.ts`

```ts
import { Client } from "pg";
import type { VercelRequest, VercelResponse } from "@vercel/node";

const fallback = [
  { time: "8:00 PM", route: "Red Line", requests: 8, mood: 3.2 },
  { time: "8:30 PM", route: "Red Line", requests: 12, mood: 2.9 },
  { time: "9:00 PM", route: "Gold Line", requests: 9, mood: 3.3 },
  { time: "9:30 PM", route: "Blue Line", requests: 14, mood: 2.7 },
  { time: "10:00 PM", route: "Red Line", requests: 19, mood: 2.6 },
  { time: "10:30 PM", route: "Bus Route 40", requests: 11, mood: 3.0 },
];

export default async function handler(
  _req: VercelRequest,
  res: VercelResponse,
) {
  if (!process.env.DATABASE_URL) {
    return res.status(200).json({ data: fallback, source: "fallback" });
  }

  const client = new Client({
    connectionString: process.env.DATABASE_URL,
    ssl: { rejectUnauthorized: false },
  });

  try {
    await client.connect();

    const result = await client.query(`
      SELECT
        TO_CHAR(time_bucket('30 minutes', created_at), 'HH12:MI AM') AS time,
        route,
        COUNT(*)::int AS requests,
        ROUND(AVG(mood_score)::numeric, 1) AS mood
      FROM community_checkins
      WHERE created_at >= NOW() - INTERVAL '12 hours'
      GROUP BY time_bucket('30 minutes', created_at), route
      ORDER BY time_bucket('30 minutes', created_at)
    `);

    return res.status(200).json({
      data: result.rows,
      source: "tiger-data",
    });
  } catch (error) {
    console.error("Community pulse error", error);
    return res.status(200).json({ data: fallback, source: "fallback" });
  } finally {
    await client.end().catch(() => undefined);
  }
}
```

## Optional: Backboard and Presage

Do these only after the main submission is safe and deployed.

### Backboard: optional feature

Backboard is positioned as an API for persistent AI memory and conversational applications; its quickstart begins with obtaining an API key from the dashboard.[^2_12][^2_13]

Use it only for a small feature:

> **My Commute Preferences**

It remembers user-selected preferences, such as:

- Preferred support style
- Favorite grounding exercise
- Common route
- Whether they prefer quiet-company matches
- Trusted-contact check-in preference

For a hackathon, this is optional because Auth0 user metadata plus your own database may already cover the same product need.

**Do not** add Backboard if it delays Gemini, ElevenLabs, Auth0, or deployment.

### Presage: optional feature

Do not build camera-based stress detection for the main demo. If you attempt it, make it an explicit-consent concept card:

> “Optional wellness check-in: users may opt into a private, on-device experience that suggests a grounding exercise. It is not a safety detector, diagnosis, or emergency trigger.”

Do not claim emotion inference is reliable, and do not use it to determine risk.

### Solana: skip

The core story is privacy, trust, peer support, and transit well-being. Wallets, transactions, and blockchain identity make the MVP harder, add friction, and create more privacy concerns. Submit a strong project to four or five prizes instead of a shallow project to nine.
